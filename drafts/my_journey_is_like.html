A reading

my journey is like—

A drawing made in five to ten minutes while feeling unpleasant, and the structure it turned out to contain.

The author was not trying to draw a life. The author was feeling unpleasant and thinking about how being alive means being stuck with what you have already done. Every stroke of the pen commits you. There is no erasing. Every morning you wake up dealt the past you did not fully choose.

The drawing turned out to enact that condition. Each new line had to negotiate with every line already on the page. What follows is a reading of how that negotiation proceeded, from the first loop to the final stop.

i.

the spiral

The drawing opens with a spiral, the line tracing its own previous orbit at an expanding radius. This is a special kind of move. It is not sympathy and it is not opposition, because there is nothing yet for the line to be in relation to. The spiral is the line generating its own prior to respond to. Each loop creates the precedent that the next loop will honor with a difference. This is where the entire logic of self-reference begins. Before you have a prior, you have to make one.

And it has a feeling that is hard to remember from outside it. From inside the spiral, you do not experience yourself as orbiting. You experience the orbit as totality. Each loop is fresh because you cannot quite see that it is a loop. Childhood does this. The patterns feel like the way reality is, not like specific patterns. You only learn they were patterns much later, often from somebody else's drawing.

The spiral cannot continue. The page is finite. So the line must either break the spiral and start to wander, or deform it — keep spiraling but lopsidedly, distorting to fit the room that remains. In the worst case, it goes off the page entirely. Most childhoods end via some mixture of the first two. The third is rare and usually means something went badly wrong.

The first grief in the drawing is here, though it is not experienced as grief. The world is finite. You are going to have to leave the spiral.

ii.

the thicket

After the spiral breaks, the line enters a high-frequency zone. It does not yet know what to do. It tries small motions, doubles back, hesitates. There is no settled pattern of continuation or refusal yet, because there is no settled prior to continue from or refuse.

This is the zone we will call adolescence, and it is where the editorial events concentrate. The line draws something detailed, and then draws a smoother envelope around it. Each smoothing is a hypothesis about which features of the line's own past are the real shape and which are texture — a real-time act of authorship about a self that is still being negotiated.

You are editing what counts as your past while you are making your present. That is exhausting in a particular way — the kind of tired that does not fix itself with sleep.

And the deepest illusion of the zone is that you have more time than you do. The intricate finding-yourself work is paced as if the drawing were enormous. In fact, much of the page has already been spent by the spiral, and the patterns laid down here turn out to be surprisingly sticky.

iii.

the committed maneuvers

Eventually the line settles into a stable enough self-conception that it can begin to have a relationship to its own past, rather than constantly redefining it. From this point on, the line has a vocabulary of moves available to it. Each one is a different way of being in relation to what has already been drawn.

sympathy and opposition

Sympathy is the new line tracing the prior at a constant distance. The shape continues; the gap stays steady. Opposition is the new line mirroring the prior — where prior bends in, new bends in; where prior bends out, new bends out — producing a gap that pinches and balloons. Both are forms of fidelity. The first is continuation; the second is reaction, which is its own kind of being-in-relation. You cannot be in opposition to something you are not still listening to.

smooth echo of fingers

There are two kinds of editorial smoothing, and they are different acts. The first — smoothing wiggles — was the texture-resolution choice of the adolescent thicket. The second is bigger. A finger is not a texture but an excursion: an entire detour the line made and returned from. Smoothing a finger is not a choice about resolution. It is a choice about which events count as part of the official record.

In a life this is the move that lets a memoir omit the breakdown, the affair, the failed venture. It is still authorship, but it is closer to narrative editing than to editorial economy. The line of the life looks more direct than it was.

vacuum filling

There is a phenomenon that looks identical to smooth echo but means almost the opposite thing. The same final image — an outer smooth boundary with detailed content inside it — can be produced by two opposite temporal orders. You cannot tell them apart from the artifact alone. Only the artist knows which way time ran.

In smooth echo, the detail was the prior and the smoothing came later. The complexity is faithfulness to a rich past. In vacuum filling, the smooth boundary was the prior, drawn fast and confident before the territory had been claimed — and the complexity that appears later is a response to the empty room that the earlier move created. The detail is centripetal. It curls inward to fill a space the line inherited.

In a life: energetic intricate activity inside a small bounded area can read as richness. Sometimes it is. Sometimes it is the tell that the boundaries were drawn too early, and the inner self is busy filling the box rather than expanding it.

the irreducible tangle

And finally, some regions are so densely worked that the order of operations cannot be reconstructed from the artifact. Was the simplicity edited from prior chaos? Was the complexity an elaboration of a simpler frame? The drawing does not say, and neither, in this case, did the drawer.

Some pockets of a life are like that — relationships, careers, identities, episodes. Not retrospectively edited and not structurally simple. They accreted. They tangled forward into themselves until the order of operations stopped being a fact and became a story. That resistance to reading is not a failure of memory. It is a real feature of the territory.

iv.

graceful giving-up

The middle exhausts. The line slows. It begins to oscillate in long, low-frequency waves — bass wiggles. This is not the line giving up. It is the line giving up the right to make complicated moves. There is a kind of dignity in the wave remaining at all when the striving has departed. The minimum viable aliveness is still a wave. The line is not collapsing into a beeline; it is winding down in rhythm.

Near the very end, a last burst of high-frequency ambition. The line tries to make small intricate features at the corner. But the pen tip is fat; the precision required is below the instrument's resolution. The complexity the line is reaching for cannot be rendered by the tool that has come this far.

So the line stops. Not because the author was done. Because the page was done, and the pen was done, and the relationship between them produced an ending. The ambition outlives the medium by a hair. That is the gentle injustice of every real ending: something is still being said, and the instrument cannot carry it any further.

v.

the symmetry

The spiral at the beginning and the bass wiggle at the end are both low-complexity phases. The middle is where complexity blooms. The two ends look formally similar — simple, slow, regular — but they are simple for opposite reasons.

The spiral is simple because nothing has happened yet.

The bass wiggle is simple because everything has happened.

Between them is the whole arc of effort. The middle's complications are what the beginning was lacking and what the end has earned the right to relinquish. The dramatic smoothing at the end represents giving up the right to make complicated moves — which is the whole privilege the middle was constructed to exercise.

The feelings live in the transitions especially. Between spiral and thicket: vertigo, the world is wider than it looked. Between thicket and committed maneuvers: a settling that is also a closing of options. Between committed maneuvers and bass wiggle: tiredness, sometimes relief. Between bass wiggle and flourish: a last hope. Between flourish and pen-limit: the gentle injustice of having something more to say and no instrument left to say it with.

The drawing took five to ten minutes.

It was made because the author was feeling unpleasant.

It was not planned, and it was not titled until afterward.

And it turned out to be readable as a life.
