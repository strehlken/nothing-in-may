A reading of a doodle

my journey is like—

A drawing made in five to ten minutes while feeling unpleasant, and what I noticed in it afterward.

I drew this in 5 to 10 minutes.

When I drew it I was feeling unpleasant. I kept thinking about sticking with things. Because life is like that. We don't get to rewrite ourselves or our journeys. Every day we wake up, an innocent new person who is dealt the past as it is.

That's what this doodle was like. At every step I had to decide where to continue the line. I was stuck with where it had already gone. That was just done — it was at that moment. I had to make do with it.

the shape of it

spiral, thicket, middle, bass-wiggle, flourish + stop

Looking at the drawing now, it falls into five phases. The line goes through each one in order. I didn't plan that.

And here they are in the actual page, color-coded:

chapter i

the beginning

the spiral
I the beginning the spiral

The beginning is an outward spiral.

It's unsustainable. It would go off the page. Or I'd have to increase the spiral distance hugely on one side, making it really lopsided. The timing of escape is unpredictable.

chapter ii

adolescence

the thicket
II adolescence the thicket

After the spiral, there's a little intermediate period. Almost high-frequency. An intricate finding yourself. In the schema this is the thicket.

In this zone you think you're going to have way more time than you do. (In the form of patience, I guess.)

The trajectory here is all over the place. It hasn't settled into a clear pattern of sympathy or opposition. Two of my three wiggle-smoothing events happen here (more on those below).

I'm calling this zone the adolescence.

chapter iii

the middle

doubling back, in two ways
III the middle sympathy, opposition, smoothing

The middle is so complicated. It's hard to look at it and say anything that feels meaningful. I could say it looks like a face — but that's like seeing shapes in clouds. That feels like imposing a reading versus listening to what's actually in the drawing — understanding the complexity and the wiggles and fingers.

What's actually in the middle is a few moves I was making.

doubling back

Near the edges of the page, or just at random, I'd double back. Once doubled back, I'd settle into a routine of re-tracing the contours of what came before — at a distance, in a new curve.

I did this in two different ways.

sympathy

The first way: following the wave-shape. Tracing a similar shape. The gap between the old line and the new one stays roughly constant.

opposition

The second way: diametrically opposed to the prior wave-shape. Riding in perfect opposition to it. When the prior curve is closer to me, I veer closer. When it's veering away, I too am veering away. So the gap between us pinches and balloons.

As I say that, it reminds me sometimes how I am with prior versions of myself — and prior versions of life handed to me through stories or relationships.

smooth echo of wiggles

Sometimes my new path is a smooth echo of what came before. There might be fine-scale features I ignore when I make my sympathy curve. The sympathy curve may be sympathetic only to the overall contour, or envelope, or local convex hull. Not trying to fit all the high-frequency structure — just the beats.

I see this happening in three places in my drawing. Maybe four — one of them could also be considered just a new finger or protrusion, so it's a bit ambiguous.

smooth echo of fingers

Fingers are what happens when you go take a winding route and double back, your u-turn making the tip of a finger. Smoothing fingers might be different from smoothing wiggles. It's when a later path comes near a bunch of finger tips but just wiggles a bit to reflect the tips and loses the full shape of the fingers. A finger isn't fine-scale texture like the wiggles — it's an entire excursion the line made and came back from.

I marked two areas of finger-smoothing in orange.

vacuum filling

There's a different phenomenon that looks similar to smooth echo but isn't. The smooth echo is kind of like a reduction in complexity as you go along the path. But sometimes more complexity shows up later, filling a space left empty by earlier journeys.

This seems to be tied to the line doubling back too early from the edge of the page, then me deciding to fill the resulting space later, energetically, with finger-like excursions.

This happened in an area of my drawing that is so tangled that it's tough to parse what was drawn first versus what was added later.

This happens sometimes in life, especially the tangling.

chapter iv

the end

bass wiggle, flourish, stop
IV the end bass wiggle · flourish · stop

graceful giving-up

The big squiggles toward the end are an attempt to give up — gracefully.

The grace is the presence of a frequency at all — versus just being a beeline for the end. The smooth wiggles themselves, even if it's just a bass tone.

I was tired of striving.

one last flourish

Then at the very end I had one last flourish of high-frequency notes. A flash of ambition.

But I had committed. I was at the end of the page. And the precision permitted by the instrument — the pen — was insufficient to continue any further. I couldn't make recursive, ever-higher-frequency wiggles.

I felt that as a statement about how life ends with this kind of dramatic smoothing.

The drawing took five to ten minutes.

It was made because I was feeling unpleasant.

It wasn't planned, and it wasn't titled until afterward.

And it turned out to be readable.
